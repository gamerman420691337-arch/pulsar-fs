# Ordinary worker retirement custody

Ordinary Linux generation, resumed generation and synchronous preview now share their engine reservation with the launcher's owned worker-tree guard. The engine retains its own share through result completion or preview authorization/publication. The guard requires both a reaped direct child and `cgroup.events` reporting `populated 0` before releasing its share. A direct-child exit alone is insufficient. Success kills any remaining owned descendants and checks the same two conditions before returning launch evidence.

Cancellation, deadline expiry and transport errors signal the owned cgroup and direct child, then poll retirement for up to two seconds. The launcher no longer calls blocking `Child::wait`. If retirement remains unconfirmed, including an error reading population evidence, guard destruction transfers the child, cgroup and reservation to an in-process reaper. The reaper keeps signaling and checking until both conditions are confirmed. Thread creation failure intentionally leaks the retained owner rather than freeing capacity. That rare fallback is inspected in source; thread-exhaustion fault injection is not qualified.

The shared reservation includes existing RAM, worker-slot and storage claims. The guard is structurally capable of retaining a GPU-bearing reservation, but ordinary production admission remains CPU-only and actual GPU retirement is not qualified. Standalone benchmark execution creates a local resource pool/claim; it does not establish aggregate admission across separate benchmark callers. The native capture and private CUDA research launch helpers retain their separate lifetime implementations.

The [validation packet](evidence/roadmap-2026-10-01/worker-retirement/validation.json) binds source archives, gate logs and application executable identities. Four local ledger cases verify retained capacity/storage for an exited child with a populated tree, an empty tree with an unreaped child, failed population observation and confirmed release. An explicitly invoked native test creates a fresh owned delegated cgroup, observes a surviving descendant after the shell exits, then confirms tree emptiness and admission release after retirement. The default suite leaves that environment-dependent case ignored; the packet records its separate executed gate. These are local CPU process/ledger checks, not GPU, platform-wide, hardware or release qualification.

In-process custody ends when the supervising process exits. Durable restart fencing, production engine CUDA admission/launch and ownership of captured native images/devices across that lifecycle remain open. Host filesystem operations, spawn and cgroup operations can still block; this is not a hard end-to-end return deadline. Completion-transaction error recovery, portable accepted CUDA roles and all model/media/device/human/release acceptance gates remain required.

The initial application campaign failed during startup/deadline checks while elevated filesystem IO pressure was observed: 85 passed, 16 failed and one was cancelled. Its source, commands, executable identities and raw reports are retained in the packet. A fresh engine-only startup probe completed in 0.45 seconds, and the unchanged full rerun passed all 102 cases. The root cause is unproven; no production fix or relaxed timeout is claimed.
