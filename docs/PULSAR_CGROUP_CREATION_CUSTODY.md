The creator owns its resource reservation and worker-fence lease before validation or filesystem effects. It requires a worker claim covering the requested memory, positive thread capacity and an already active format-4 ledger. It captures the recorded parent directory, generates a scoped UUID leaf and durably acknowledges Intent before mkdirat through that held parent. It captures the created directory through the same parent, publishes its held identity as Bound before configuring limits, then transfers Bound into registered ownership with one journal publication. Limit writes and process-placement handle opening use the held child directory.

A successful creator moves into WorkerCustody without fallible IO. No process-placement handle is exposed by the admitted creator. Worker custody installs the group, reservation and fence before exposing a handle or spawning. The native success fixture checks the registered roster and actual memory.max, places its own /bin/true child into the group, observes/reaps it, retires the group and confirms that claims remain charged until custody is dropped.

Failures transfer the entire attempted state into the shared cleanup task. Captured roots require matching identity and emptiness, acknowledged retirement before deletion, confirmation that the held inode was unlinked, and acknowledged clear before claims release. An uncaptured existing Intent remains ambiguous: its name does not authorize deletion. A replacement refuses even when the original held inode has already been unlinked. Failed publication and deletion retain both the reservation and engine-scope lease while retrying only the owned obligation.

The native publication fixture denies writes only to its private fence directory at Intent publication, held-identity binding, registration and after acknowledged registration. Across repeated cleanup retries it checks that storage remains charged, a second worker cannot reserve capacity and another scope opener refuses. Restoring permissions permits cleanup and release. Additional cases create an owned nested cgroup that blocks deletion, inject failure immediately after mkdir before capture, and replace an unlinked captured root before binding. The ambiguous-root fixture independently observes and removes its own exact empty identity; the replacement fixture removes only its separately owned canary. Production cleanup does neither by name inference.
